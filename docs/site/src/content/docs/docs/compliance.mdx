Hikyo provides security controls that operators can use in their own compliance
program. Installing Hikyo does not establish GDPR compliance or a SOC 2 report.
This guide covers self-hosted installations. It makes no claim of certification,
attestation, or an independently assessed production deployment.

Start with the [personal-data inventory](/docs/privacy/), then use the
[operator procedures](/docs/compliance-operations/) to assign owners and collect evidence.

## Responsibility boundary

| Area | Hikyo product | Installation operator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Scoped authorization, MFA, federation and SCIM controls | Configure privileges, review access, secure administrators and remove access when people leave |
| Protection | Encrypted value storage, key rotation, TLS boundaries and encrypted archives | Protect hosts, database metadata, keys, disks, network and archive destinations |
| Evidence | Audit query/export, operational metrics and doctor findings | Preserve independent copies, investigate alerts and record reviews |
| Privacy | Existing scoped reads and lifecycle operations | Determine purposes, lawful bases, notices, rights handling and justified retention; account for the gaps below |
| Recovery | Backup scheduling, archive retention and restore drills | Maintain independent recovery material, off-host copies and exercised recovery procedures |

An operator may be a controller, processor, or both for different processing.
Determine roles from actual purposes and instructions. Distributing the software
does not by itself make its publisher the processor of a customer's installation.
Support access or hosted services would require a separate assessment.

## Controls and limits

Use the commands and configuration below with a version that includes these
controls. The [implementation status](/implementation-status/) remains the
capability-status authority; these docs track the main branch.

| Control | Product behavior | Operator responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity lifecycle | Host-local subject export, correction, restriction, release, erasure and restore receipts | Review export coverage and legal grounds; inspect customer content and external recipients separately |
| Audit expiry | Bounded access/security policies, audited adoption and transactional pruning | Choose justified periods before upgrade and retain necessary independent evidence |
| Content lifecycle | Revision GC plus explicit whole-environment deletion | Age OR revision count is not an absolute privacy deadline; review scope before deleting content |
| Independent audit evidence | Append-only application records and export; doctor evidence collection | Database operators can alter records; protect off-host copies and monitor collection |
| Backup reconciliation | Identity action receipts can be reapplied after restore | Keep current receipts outside archives, apply them before reopening service and manage all external copies |

Follow [identity workflows](/docs/privacy/#identity-workflows),
[audit retention](/docs/privacy/#audit-retention) and
[evidence collection](/docs/compliance-operations/#access-review-and-evidence).
Neither an identity export nor its erasure receipt claims to cover arbitrary
personal data inside values, audit payloads, project metadata or third-party systems.

## SOC 2 scope and evidence

SOC 2 examines a service organization's defined system. Type I addresses control
design at a specified date; Type II also examines operating effectiveness over a
specified period. The applicable criteria and evidence depend on the system and
commitments agreed with the auditor. For a secrets service, consider Security and
Confidentiality, with Availability where recovery commitments warrant it.

Keep a control register with: criterion, owner, implementation, operating procedure,
evidence location, review date and exceptions. Include organizational controls
such as risk assessment, personnel access, supplier review, incident handling,
change approval and vulnerability remediation. Product tests alone cannot prove
that these controls operated throughout an audit period.

See the [AICPA SOC 2 overview](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2/)
and [Trust Services Criteria](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rb9cdnjh59cm/72xv4p67HVXKp6CjWmjkPk/1cdbfa19f6307e2720396b66a6194dc9/trust-services-criteria-updated-copyright.pdf).

## GDPR scope

Identify purposes and lawful bases, provide notices, and record retention and
recipients. Determine requirements for processing records, processor agreements,
international transfers, impact assessments and a data protection officer from
your processing circumstances. EU hosting alone does not settle these questions.
Encryption does not turn identifiable personal data into anonymous data.

Use [GDPR Articles 5, 6, 13, 14, 28, 30, 32, 35, 37 and Chapter V](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)
as the legal source and have the responsible privacy adviser validate the decisions.

## Future hosted service

Before offering managed hosting, extend the same inventory and control register
to infrastructure, support access, customer offboarding, subprocessors, contracts,
service commitments and breach communications. Verify isolation and privacy
operations across tenants. Obtain an assessment scoped to that service before
making assurance claims; self-hosted documentation is not a hosted-service report.
